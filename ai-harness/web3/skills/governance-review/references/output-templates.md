# Output templates

Choose by what the user asked for. Keep headings short; prose inside sections.

## A. Structured review (default, private)

**Verdict:** Support / Support with changes / Oppose / Cannot assess yet — one sentence why.
**Strongest objection:** one paragraph.
**Context:** DAO, proposal type, process stage, user's stake/conflicts.
**Findings:** grouped Blocking → Major → Minor. Each: section reference, what's wrong, why it matters (failure mechanism), fix or question.
**Numbers checked:** recomputed figures and discrepancies.
**On-chain verification:** what was checked, results, what wasn't checked.
**Open questions for the author.**
**What would change the verdict.**

## B. Forum reviewer reply (public)

1. Disclosure line if any conflict exists.
2. One-paragraph summary of position.
3. Numbered concerns, most serious first — each with a concrete ask.
4. What the reviewer does like (only if genuine; one or two sentences).
5. Closing: conditions for support.
Tone: respectful, specific, no rhetorical questions or sarcasm. Length: as short as the concerns allow.

## C. Vote rationale

Vote (For / Against / Abstain), 2–5 sentences of reasoning tied to the decisive factors, disclosure if relevant, and what would have changed the vote. Suitable for delegate platforms and forum threads.

## D. Draft feedback (author mode)

**Readiness:** Ready to post / Needs revision / Not ready — why.
**Blocking gaps** before posting.
**Section-by-section edits:** location → suggested change (show rewritten text only where wording is the issue).
**Anticipated objections** and whether the draft answers them.
**Consistency check** of figures, dates, addresses.
Offer to produce a revised full draft only after the user confirms the changes.

## E. Red-team memo

For adversarial stress-testing (including of the user's own organisation's proposals): assume a motivated opponent and a motivated attacker. Sections: how the proposal could be exploited; how it fails under stress; how a critic would frame it publicly; conflicts of interest; mitigations ranked by impact vs effort.
